\section{Problem and Product Grounding}

\subsection{The User and the Problem}

The primary target users for this system are operations directors of mid-sized pharmaceutical distribution warehouses in China. A typical user oversees a facility processing approximately 2,000 orders daily across 42,000 SKUs, with strict temperature requirements spanning ambient, cool, cold, and frozen categories. Every morning at 7:00 AM, the operations team gathers around a whiteboard to manually group orders into ``waves'' (batches) for coordinated picking. The rules they use are simple: group by temperature zone first, then by geographic proximity. They have relied on this manual heuristic for years.

This manual process is increasingly untenable due to three converging forces:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{SKU complexity}: 38\% of SKUs are near-expiry managed items with varying temperature requirements; cross-zone contamination risks severe GSP (Good Supply Practice) regulatory violations\parencite{paxafe2025ai}.
    \item \textbf{Order fragmentation}: Daily active customers exceed 1,200, mostly requiring small-batch orders averaging 3--5 items, making manual grouping cognitively demanding.
    \item \textbf{Peak volatility}: During flu seasons and promotional periods, order volume spikes to 280\% of the baseline, overwhelming the whiteboard method entirely.
\end{enumerate}

However, the ultimate barrier to solving this crisis is not algorithmic, but organizational. Most enterprise AI fails to be adopted not because the math is wrong, but because the cost of \textit{trying} it is too high. An operations director does not have a three-month IT budget or the political capital to pilot a black-box AI algorithm. What they need is a way to see, in exactly five minutes, whether AI-driven wave allocation would actually save money---before committing to any integration.

\subsection{Product Positioning: The Risk Reversal Engine}

\textbf{Maverick-SORT} is not designed to replace an existing Warehouse Management System (WMS). It is an intelligent decision-support sandbox. Its core philosophy is \textbf{Risk Reversal}: allowing decision-makers to quantify the ROI of AI-driven scheduling without touching live production systems or requiring IT department approval.

To address the adoption bottleneck, the product delivers three core business values, structured into a deliberate commercial escalation path:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Zero-Risk ROI Validation (Essential Edition)}: Built as a zero-deployment, browser-based tool, the Essential tier reduces a heavy ``organizational evaluation project'' to a five-minute ``What-If'' scenario simulation. It allows managers to test parameters (e.g., changing wave capacity from 20 to 15) on demo data and instantly view the projected cost savings, lowering the perceived risk of adoption to near zero.
    
    \item \textbf{Explainable AI for GSP Compliance (Shared Core)}: In the heavily regulated pharmaceutical sector, black-box routing algorithms are un-deployable. Maverick-SORT places a Knowledge-Guided Deep Reinforcement Learning (KGDRL) core alongside several traditional heuristics in an ``Algorithm Arena.'' This transparently exposes the trade-offs between picking efficiency and GSP temperature compliance, keeping the human operations director firmly in the loop.
    
    \item \textbf{Scalable Enterprise Escalation (Pro Edition)}: Once trust is established via the Essential tier, the Pro Edition provides the tools for operational scale. It introduces an EWMA-based dynamic capacity adapter for peak volatility, comprehensive RAG root-cause analysis, and an NSGA-II multi-objective strategy optimizer, transforming the sandbox into a live command center.
\end{itemize}

The value proposition behind this two-tiered strategy is singular and highly disciplined: \textit{verify the algorithmic performance and compliance safety first, commit to the multi-objective enterprise scale second.}

Although the original casebook also suggested inventory scheduling and near-expiry management functionalities, we intentionally concentrated on wave allocation and simulation because these represent the highest operational bottleneck and could be developed into a functioning prototype within the course timeframe.

\subsection{Guiding Thesis and Question}

This document asks: \textbf{What most limits Maverick-SORT from reaching real use by pharmaceutical warehouse operators, and what does building it teach us about working with AI?}

A central thesis emerged throughout the project: \textbf{the primary bottleneck to enterprise AI adoption is not algorithm quality, but organizational attention.}

The challenge is not whether the technology works. Rather, it is whether an already overloaded operations manager has the time, attention, and confidence to evaluate a new system. This insight shaped every major design decision, from the browser-based Essential Edition to the five-minute What-If simulator and free-trial model.

The sections that follow examine how this adoption bottleneck influenced both the product design and our experience of building with AI.
